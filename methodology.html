Signal Methodology

Three horizons, one glance.

Markets rarely move in one direction at every timescale at once. A rally that looks strong today can sit inside a multi-month downtrend — and knowing which is which changes the trade entirely. Our signals separate those horizons instead of collapsing them into a single verdict.

Short-Term

Days

Reads the immediate tape: momentum, session ranges, and how volatility is behaving right now. This is the fastest-moving of the three and will flip more often than the others by design.

RedYellowGreen

Three states — the yellow middle exists because short-horizon conditions are frequently genuinely undecided, and forcing a call there would be noise dressed as conviction.

Medium-Term

Weeks

Tracks the prevailing swing — whether the market is building on its moves or reversing them. Slower to change, and it ignores the day-to-day chop the short-term signal responds to.

RedGreen

Two states only. Over a multi-week window the trend is either intact or it isn't; a neutral option here would mostly be an excuse not to commit.

Long-Term

Months

The structural backdrop. Changes rarely, and when it does the shift usually matters — this is the horizon that frames whether shorter-term signals are trading with the tide or against it.

RedYellowGreen

Three states — major turns take time to confirm, and yellow marks the stretch where the old regime has weakened but a new one hasn't established itself.

Reading Them Together

The three signals are most useful in combination. A few of the configurations worth recognizing:

Aligned

All three agree. The cleanest backdrop — short-term strength is supported rather than fighting the larger structure.

Counter-Trend Bounce

Short-term turns up while the larger horizons stay negative. Rallies here have historically been shorter-lived and quicker to reverse.

Pause In An Uptrend

The structure holds while the near term consolidates — often the signal doing the least is the one worth respecting.

Structure Weakening

Faster horizons have already turned and the long-term signal has lost its footing. Historically the configuration that precedes regime change.

How the Signals Are Built

Each horizon runs its own model over its own lookback window, drawing on price action, trading ranges, volatility behaviour, and market participation. The outputs are condensed into a single state per horizon — the point is a decision you can read in under a second, not a dashboard that needs interpreting.

The system is powered by proprietary algorithms developed through a synergy of advanced AI and seasoned derivatives experts with decades of market experience. The models are reviewed on an ongoing basis; the specific indicators, weightings, and thresholds are not published.

What These Signals Are Not

They are not price targets, entry or exit instructions, or forecasts of where the index will be on any given date. They describe the market's current posture over a given horizon — nothing more.

No signal system is right every time, and every one of these will be wrong at some point. Position sizing, risk limits, and the decision to act at all remain yours.
